Before you start

Why we do this

GroundWorks tells us when we are about to run out, so we reorder in time and nobody finds an empty shelf in the back during a rush. It only works if the count matches what is really there. You have four small jobs that keep it right.

Job 1

Move stock out

When a unit leaves the storeroom for the floor, tap − (unless sales sync does it).

Job 2

Log deliveries

When an order arrives, add it to the count.

Job 3

Count at close

Walk the storage areas and type in what is really there.

Job 4

Check what to order

The Order tab lists what is running low.

How your shop counts

Two ways. Your manager picks one.

Storeroom counting

We count what is in the back

The count is the stock in the storeroom, walk-in and back shelves. When a unit goes out to the floor, you tap −. Whatever is open or out on the floor is not tracked. It gets used within a day or two.

Used by shops that enter stock by hand.

Sales sync is on

The app subtracts as we sell

The app takes ingredients off the count as drinks sell. During the shift you do nothing for those items. Your jobs are deliveries and the closing count, which catches whatever the sync missed.

Used by Pro shops with the Square connection.

Never do both for the same item.

If the app already subtracts milk from sales, tapping − on milk as well would count it twice. When you are unsure about an item, check the rules above, then ask your manager.

Step 1 · Start of shift

Open the app and read the screen

  1. Open groundworksinventory.dev on the shop phone or tablet. If it asks you to sign in, use the shop login your manager gave you.
  2. Look at the three boxes near the top. They count your items by how close each one is to running out.
  3. Tap a box to show only those items. Tap it again, or tap Show all, to see everything.
  4. To find one item fast, type its name in Find an item….
Stocked

Green: fine. You have more than the reorder level.

Reorder

Amber: running low. At or below the reorder level. It is on the Order tab.

Critical

Red: almost out. At or below half the reorder level. Tell your manager now.

Step 2 · During your shift

Move stock out, or fix a mistake

Move stock out

If your shop counts the storeroom

  1. When you take a unit out of the back to use on the floor (a fresh gallon, a new bag, a new sleeve), find the item.
  2. Tap − once. The count goes down by one.
  3. Example: you carry a fresh gallon of milk from the walk-in to the bar. Tap − once on Whole Milk. The gallon on the bar is no longer tracked.

If sales sync is on

  1. For items the app tracks, do nothing during the shift. It subtracts them as drinks sell.
  2. For items it does not track (ask your manager, for example stir sticks), use the storeroom steps above.
Only stock that leaves the back counts.

Do not tap − for pours, for refilling a pitcher, or for a half-used carton on the floor.

Got it wrong?

  1. Tap Undo on the bar at the bottom. You have about six seconds. Several taps on the same item undo together.
  2. Too late for Undo? Tap + to add one back, or tap the number itself and type the right count, like 3.5 for three and a half gallons. Press Enter.
Step 3 · A delivery arrives

Add it to the count

  1. Find the item that came in and tap Delivery under it. A form opens right under that item. Deliveries go into the back count, so log them before the stock goes out to the floor.
  2. Type how many came in. For milk, that is how many gallons.
  3. Milk, cream and other fresh items: tap Add with expiry date, then pick the date printed on the package. Add a note like "Tuesday delivery" if you like. The app then uses the oldest date first.
  4. Everything else: leave it on Just add to stock.
  5. Check the line that says New total, then tap Add.
  6. If what arrived is a different size than usual, for example half-gallons instead of gallons, tap Different size this time? and enter the size, or ask your manager.
Need to see or fix an expiry date later?

Tap Expiry under a fresh item. It lists each delivery with its date, and the one that expires first says "Use first".

Step 4 · Closing count

Count what is really on the shelf

  1. Near the end of the day, tap Closing Count.
  2. Walk your storage areas. Look at each item.
  3. Check every item. If the number in its box is right, leave it. If it is not, type the real number. Use decimals for partials, like 2.5.
  4. The bar at the bottom shows how many counts you changed.
  5. Tap Save Closing Count once. You will see "Closing count saved."

What to count

  • Storeroom counting: count only what is in the back (storeroom, walk-in, back shelves). Do not count what is open or out on the floor.
  • Sales sync is on: count everything, including the floor. For an open carton or bag, round to the nearest quarter: 0.25, 0.5 or 0.75.
Why this matters.

With storeroom counting, it keeps the back count honest. With sales sync, it is the safety net that catches spills, waste and anything sold outside the register.

Did not mean to start?

Tap Quick adjust to leave without saving. Nothing changes until you save.

Step 5 · What to order

Check the Order tab

  1. Tap Order at the top. The small orange number on it is how many items need ordering.
  2. The list shows what is low, most urgent first, with how many to order.
  3. Once someone has placed the order, tap Ordered ✓ on that item so nobody orders it twice. It moves to "Already ordered" for everyone on shift.
  4. Need to send the list to your manager or supplier? Tap Share list.
You do not have to place the order yourself.

Follow your shop's rule for who orders. When the delivery arrives and you log it, the "ordered" mark clears by itself.

Step 6 · Text alerts

If a text arrives

Your manager gets a text when an item gets worse, for example from Stocked to Reorder Soon, or from Reorder Soon to Critical. It sends one text per change, not a stream.

Example text
GroundWorks Inventory: Half & Half is now Critical — 1 carton left (threshold 3). Reply STOP to opt out.
What to do.

Open the Order tab. If the item is already marked ordered, you are done. If not, tell whoever places orders.

Step 7 · Owner PIN

If the app asks for a PIN

Some things are for the owner or manager only: editing an item, adding a new item, Recipes, Alerts and billing. If the shop has turned the PIN on, you will see a padlock on the Recipes and Alerts tabs, and a box asking for the PIN when you try the others.

  1. Tap Cancel. You do not need those settings for your jobs.
  2. If something truly needs changing, ask your manager.
  3. Do not guess the PIN. After five wrong tries it locks for five minutes.

Counting, using, deliveries, closing count and the Order tab always work without a PIN.
